## Book Review : Bite-Size Linux by Julia Evans

**Author :** Julia Evans  
**Genre :** Computer Science / Linux  
**Rating :** ⭐⭐⭐⭐ (4/5)

### About the book

*Bite-Size Linux* lays the foundations for understanding a Linux system : processes, files, users, permissions, and how it all fits together. As always with Julia Evans, the technical subject is cut into digestible bites, easy to swallow even for beginners.

### What I liked

- **Clear explanations** : every concept is explained with the *why* behind the *how*.
- **Made for beginners and seasoned users alike** : a good refresher even when you already touch Linux.
- **Bite-size format** : perfect for picking up concepts between work sessions.
- **Concrete examples** : the commands and concepts are directly testable in a real terminal.
- **The drawings** : Julia Evans' illustrations are perfect! They completely break away from the monochrome routine of other technical articles and books — think No Starch Press editions — with color and whimsy from the very start, all while staying professional and accessible.

### What I liked less

- We could have done with an **even longer** book once you get a taste of it!
- Some advanced concepts remain **skipped over** for seasoned users.

### Verdict

An excellent introduction to Linux for **anyone who wants to understand their system instead of using it as a black box**. Quick to read, easy to remember, and directly applicable.

**Recommended if :** you want to finally understand what happens under the hood of your machine, without drowning in dry technical tomes.
