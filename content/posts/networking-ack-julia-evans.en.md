## Book Review : Networking ACK by Julia Evans

**Author :** Julia Evans  
**Genre :** Computer Science / Networking  
**Rating :** ⭐⭐⭐⭐ (4/5)

### About the book

*Networking ACK* explores the inner workings of the network : how packets travel, what ACKs really mean, timeouts, retransmissions, and how to diagnose a picky network. A notoriously dry subject that Julia Evans finally makes approachable.

### What I liked

- **Clear explanations** : the network becomes crystal clear instead of a magical black box.
- **Made for beginners and seasoned users alike** : a welcome refresher for any dev working with networks.
- **Bite-size format** : perfect for picking up concepts between two `ping` and `tcpdump` sessions.
- **Concrete examples** : concepts are tied to real debugging situations, directly applicable day-to-day.
- **The drawings** : Julia Evans' illustrations are perfect! They completely break away from the monochrome routine of other technical articles and books — think No Starch Press editions — with color and whimsy from the very start, all while staying professional and accessible.

### What I liked less

- We could have done with an **even longer** book once you get a taste of it!
- The most complex protocols remain **skipped over** for seasoned network engineers.

### Verdict

An excellent read for **understanding why your connection lags, what an ACK does, and how to debug a network that's not working**. Finally, real networking explained in color and without useless jargon.

**Recommended if :** you've ever wandered between "why does it only work on my machine?" and "why `Connection timed out`?"
